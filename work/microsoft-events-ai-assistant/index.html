Data uncovered a low-value Chatbot that made us rethink the friction behind AI Assistant experience

Microsoft event websites are information-rich experiences. While an AI Assistant (a standard chatbot) is available, both quantitative and qualitative data consistently showed low adoption and even lower perceived value.

255k# of registered attendees
50k# of chatbot users
81k# of questions answered
2.6kAnswers liked
1.4kAnswers disliked

I conducted a 7-question survey to confirm assumptions made from past research and discover new insights.

To ensure our efforts were strategically aligned, I first engaged with key stakeholders to learn their curiosities, hypotheses, and the data they needed to see. I then created a short and open-ended survey to learn attendee needs and pain-points.

I synthesized 1000+ verbatim into Impact-Effort Matrix, identifying key themes and emerging opportunities.

The survey had 156 responses, providing valuable data on attendee behaviors, expectations, and frustrations with the AI Assistant. I used Impact-Effort Matrix to prioritize potential AI Assistant features and Decision Log for implementation to ensure a feasible development.

Research pushed us move beyond chatbot fixes to built a true AI Assistant for automated event experience

The research revealed that attendees needed more than a reactive chatbot. Therefore I changed my strategy to design a true AI Assistant capable of making the complex event experience seamless and intuitive.

HMW design AI-powered attendee experience that is personalized, assisted, and automated.

Design Decision No.1

Driving discoverability and increasing awareness

Pain point 1: hidden in plain sight, failed to stand out

The AI Assistant’s main entry point is easy to miss

The AI Assistant’s main entry point “Customize your schedule” has low adoption. Only 19% of attendees used it, and over half said they didn’t even know it existed. Heat map confirmed AI Assistant is one of the least popular click on the home page.

CTA is overshadowed by more visually dominant content

Solution 1

Solution 1: design hierarchy to elevate the AI Assistant as a primary action

I made the entry point feel like a key part of the planning journey. Early usability tests showed increased click-throughs and reduced hesitation time to engage with the AI Assistant.

Simple yet persistent CTA that’s visually distinct

Pain point 2: unclear messaging so no reason to engage

The chatbot has no clear value proposition

The chatbot has no clear messaging about what it could do or how it would save time. The AI Assistant never earned their buy-in.

The chatbot’s capabilities are unclear, leaving attendees unsure of the next step.

Solution 2

Solution 2: use persuasive prompts to give attendees a reason to care

The chatbot now surfaces relevant prompts to give attendees a clear starting point. This gave attendees a clear reason to engage and helped them see real value quickly.

Clear prompts for solving valuable problems

Design Decision No.2

Simplifying the overwhelming pre-event planning

Too many filter options leads to information overload

Building a schedule was time-consuming and cognitively demanding

Attendees had to browse pages of sessions and add them one-by-one to their schedule. Research confirmed this time-consuming and cognitively-demanding task hurt pre-event satisfaction.

Endless browsing and extensive scrolling · Separate clicks create high friction and repetitive task

Solution 1

Solution 1: automating planning to reduce friction

A full-page AI flow that generates a personalized schedule based on attendee profile. Attendees can review, swap, or add all sessions at once—turning a stressful process into a confident, guided experience.

AI justifications ensure best matching choice

Pain point 2: decision fatigue caused missed opportunities

Overlapping sessions made planning stressful

Overlapping sessions made planning stressful, and without AI support, resolving conflicts was error-prone. Attendees lacked confidence they picked the right sessions for their specific level, background, or needs.

Lacking clear guidance or actionable solutions to decide “which one is better?” or “what should I do?”

Solution 2

Solution 2: smart conflict resolution that builds confidence

The AI Assistant now recommends the best-fit options with clearing reasoning based on input. Attendees feel confident in their choices and can build a strong schedule in minutes, not hours.

Session comparison to simplify decision

Design Decision No.3

Connecting attendees with the right people

Pain point 1: no direction, no connection

Attendees don’t know who to talk to

Attendees don’t know who to talk to. The current directory provides surface-level info offers no meaningful way to identify relevant people, people are left guessing who’s worth reaching out to.

Networking felt random, high-effort, and often unproductive. Hard to identify who’s like-minded or where to start.

Solution 1

Solution 1: help attendees find “their people”

AI Assistant makes networking no longer a guesswork by surfacing relevant contacts based on shared sessions and background.

Tags and shared sessions help quickly assess background fit.

Pain point 2: the UI assumes everyone is an extrovert

Cold outreach creates a barrier for introverts

The current CTAs to connect require attendees to initiate cold outreach, which majority won’t do. This creates a barrier for introverts and first-timers, who want connection but need help breaking the ice.

Lacking visibility into availability or responsiveness · No facilitation to avoid awkward cold start

Solution 2

Solution 2: facilitate low-pressure networking

Designed structured prompts, conversation starters, or shared group chats based on session attendance or topics of interest. Using real-time context to identify who’s actively open to networking.

Improved nudges drove more reach outs.

The revamped AI Assistant empowers attendees and drives business outcomes
